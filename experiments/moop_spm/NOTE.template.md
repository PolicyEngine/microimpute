# How the MOOP imputation method moves SPM poverty

The American Community Survey (ACS) does not ask about medical out-of-pocket spending (MOOP), so researchers who build a Supplemental Poverty Measure (SPM) on the ACS impute it. For 2014, Census researchers imputed it with predicted mean matching by insurance status, and subtracting their imputed MOOP raised the ACS SPM rate by 4.57 percentage points. In the Current Population Survey's Annual Social and Economic Supplement (CPS ASEC), which asks about MOOP, subtracting it raised the rate by 3.48 points, though the two surveys subtracted similar totals of $561 billion and $567 billion ([Fox, Pacas and Glassman, 2017](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/working-papers/2017/demo/Fox-ACSSPM2014.pdf), slides 10, 11 and 26). The surveys also differ in income and other resources, so we test the imputation alone: we impute MOOP within the CPS ASEC and check each method against the file's own MOOP.

## Data and design

We use the {n_persons} people, {n_units} SPM units and {n_households} households in the 2026 CPS ASEC public-use file, which covers calendar-year 2025. We impute each unit's SPM medical expenses (`SPM_MEDXPNS`), its members' MOOP plus their Medicare premiums. Census subtracts them from SPM resources dollar for dollar, so an imputed value moves resources against an unchanged threshold. With reported MOOP and the ASEC person weight, we compute an SPM rate of {rate2_all_reported}% with a 90% margin of error of {reported_moe90} points; Census published {published_rate}% and {published_moe90} in [P60-290](https://www.census.gov/library/publications/2026/demo/p60-290.html). Subtracting MOOP raises the rate by {moop_effect} points (SE {moop_effect_se}), and Census reports {published_moop_effect}.

We limit every method to predictors an ACS-style file carries: members' ages, sex, disability and current health coverage, the unit's cash income on its own and relative to its SPM threshold, family type, tenure and state. We assign whole households to {folds} folds and impute each fold from models we fit on the rest, without survey weights. We average {draws} draws for each stochastic method and compute standard errors from the 160 replicate weights, treating each fitted model as fixed.

- **(a) Logit plus means.** A logistic model predicts whether a unit has any MOOP. Each unit with a predicted probability of 0.5 or more gets the mean MOOP of donor spenders in its cell, or an OLS prediction among spenders; the rest get zero. Cells cross coverage group, head's age, cash income relative to the threshold and unit size.
- **(b) Logit draws plus donors.** The same model sets participation by a random draw, and each spender takes a random donor spender's MOOP from its cell.
- **(c) QRF and ZI-QRF.** Quantile regression forests from PolicyEngine's microimpute package, alone and inside its zero-inflated wrapper, give each unit a random quantile of its predicted distribution. PolicyEngine's microimpute [paper](../../paper/main.pdf) found that quantile regression forests had the lowest average quantile loss of five methods for imputing Survey of Consumer Finances (SCF) net worth onto the CPS.
- **PMM.** Predictive mean matching on log MOOP within five coverage groups, modeled on the Census researchers' approach.

Every method moves people across the line in both directions. QRF moves {newly_poor_qrf}% of people into poverty and {newly_nonpoor_qrf}% out of it, and logit plus cell means moves {newly_poor_logit_cell_mean}% in and {newly_nonpoor_logit_cell_mean}% out. Census researchers turned to the ACS for state and substate SPM estimates, where these flows need not offset.

Census imputes MOOP for {share_persons_moop_allocated}% of the people in the file, so its own imputation supplies part of the benchmark. In units where Census imputes no member's MOOP, every method's change rises: to {subset_diff_logit_cell_mean} points for logit plus cell means, {subset_diff_pmm} for PMM and {subset_diff_qrf} for QRF (SE {subset_se_qrf}). Our donors and recipients share one survey's questions, income measures and population, so the test leaves out the differences between surveys that an ACS imputation also faces.
