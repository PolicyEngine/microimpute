# Supplemental Security Income (SSI): A Policy Overview

## 1. What SSI Is

### Purpose

Supplemental Security Income (SSI) is a federal means-tested income
assistance program administered by the Social Security Administration
(SSA). It provides monthly cash payments to aged, blind, and disabled
individuals who have limited income and resources. Unlike Social
Security retirement and disability benefits, SSI is not based on prior
work history or payroll tax contributions. Instead, it is funded
through general tax revenues from the U.S. Treasury
([SSA, 2023](https://www.ssa.gov/ssi)).

SSI serves as the nation's primary cash safety net for the most
economically vulnerable Americans who are aged or have disabilities,
functioning as a program of last resort for individuals who cannot
support themselves through employment and lack sufficient Social
Security or other benefits.

### History

SSI was established by Title XVI of the Social Security Act and began
making payments on January 1, 1974. The program replaced three
earlier federal-state programs:

- **Old-Age Assistance (OAA)** -- aid for the elderly poor
- **Aid to the Blind (AB)** -- assistance for blind individuals
- **Aid to the Permanently and Totally Disabled (APTD)** -- support
  for people with disabilities

Prior to SSI, these programs were administered by individual states,
leading to significant variation in eligibility criteria, benefit
levels, and administrative practices. Congress created SSI to
establish a uniform, nationwide floor of income support with
consistent federal eligibility standards and benefit amounts
([Congressional Research Service, 2022](https://crsreports.congress.gov/product/pdf/RL/RL32279)).

### Policy Goals

The core policy goals of SSI include:

1. **Poverty alleviation**: Providing a minimum income floor for
   aged, blind, and disabled individuals who lack other means of
   support.
2. **Nationwide uniformity**: Replacing the patchwork of state
   programs with consistent federal eligibility rules and benefit
   levels.
3. **Dignity and independence**: Enabling recipients to meet basic
   needs such as food and shelter.
4. **Administrative efficiency**: Centralizing program administration
   under the Social Security Administration.

## 2. Who Qualifies for SSI

### Categorical Eligibility

An applicant must meet one of the following categorical requirements
([SSA, 2023](https://www.ssa.gov/benefits/ssi/eligibility)):

- **Aged**: Age 65 or older.
- **Blind**: Visual acuity of 20/200 or less in the better eye with
  use of a correcting lens, or a visual field limitation such that
  the widest diameter subtends an angle no greater than 20 degrees.
- **Disabled**: Inability to engage in any substantial gainful
  activity (SGA) by reason of a medically determinable physical or
  mental impairment that can be expected to result in death or that
  has lasted or can be expected to last for a continuous period of
  not less than 12 months. For children under age 18, the standard
  is having a medically determinable impairment that results in
  marked and severe functional limitations.

### Income Limits

SSI uses a broad definition of income that includes both earned and
unearned income. Key exclusions and disregards include
([20 CFR § 416.1112](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/416.1112)):

- **General income exclusion**: The first $20 per month of most
  unearned income (or earned income if no unearned income).
- **Earned income exclusion**: The first $65 per month of earned
  income, plus one-half of remaining earned income.
- **Student earned income exclusion**: Up to $2,220 per month (with
  an annual maximum of $8,950) for students under age 22 in 2023.

The substantial gainful activity (SGA) level for non-blind disabled
individuals was $1,470 per month in 2023; for blind individuals, it
was $2,460 per month
([SSA, 2023](https://www.ssa.gov/oact/cola/sga.html)).

### Resource Limits

Applicants must have countable resources below
([20 CFR § 416.1205](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/416.1205)):

- **$2,000** for an individual
- **$3,000** for a couple

These limits have not been adjusted for inflation since 1989. Notable
resource exclusions include:

- The home the individual lives in (regardless of value)
- One vehicle (generally)
- Household goods and personal effects
- Life insurance with a face value of $1,500 or less per person
- Burial funds up to $1,500 per person

### Citizenship and Residency Requirements

Applicants generally must be:

- A U.S. citizen or national, **or** in a qualified alien category
  (e.g., lawful permanent residents meeting certain conditions,
  refugees, asylees, certain trafficking victims)
- A resident of one of the 50 states, DC, or the Northern Mariana
  Islands
- Not absent from the country for 30 or more consecutive days

## 3. Benefit Amounts

### Federal Benefit Rate (FBR) for 2023

The FBR is the maximum monthly federal SSI payment. For 2023
([SSA](https://www.ssa.gov/oact/cola/SSIamts.html)):

| Recipient Category   | Monthly FBR | Annual FBR  |
|----------------------|-------------|-------------|
| Eligible individual  | $914        | $10,968     |
| Eligible couple      | $1,371      | $16,452     |

The 2023 COLA was 8.7%, one of the largest increases in decades,
reflecting high inflation in 2022.

### How Benefits Are Calculated

The SSI benefit formula
([42 U.S.C. § 1382](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/1382)):

**SSI Federal Benefit = FBR - Countable Income**

Where countable income is determined after applying all applicable
exclusions. For example, for an individual with $300/month in Social
Security and no earned income:

1. Start with unearned income: $300
2. Subtract general exclusion: $300 - $20 = $280
3. Countable income = $280
4. SSI benefit = $914 - $280 = **$634/month**

For earned income of $500/month with no unearned income:

1. Subtract general exclusion: $500 - $20 = $480
2. Subtract earned income exclusion: $480 - $65 = $415
3. Exclude 50% of remainder: $415 / 2 = $207.50
4. Countable income = $207.50
5. SSI benefit = $914 - $207.50 = **$706.50/month**

### State Supplements

Many states provide supplemental payments on top of the federal SSI
benefit. Approximately 44 states and DC provide some form of state
supplementation. In calendar year 2023, federally administered state
supplementation totaled $3.2 billion
([SSI Annual Statistical Report, 2023](https://www.ssa.gov/policy/docs/statcomps/ssi_asr/2023/sect01.html)).

## 4. How SSI Differs from SSDI

| Feature                 | SSI                                    | SSDI                                   |
|------------------------|----------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **Funding source**     | General tax revenues                   | Social Security trust fund             |
| **Work history**       | No work history required               | Must have sufficient work credits      |
| **Means test**         | Yes (income and resource limits)       | No means test                          |
| **Benefit basis**      | Need-based (FBR minus countable income)| Based on past earnings (AIME/PIA)      |
| **Health coverage**    | Medicaid (automatic in most states)    | Medicare (after 24-month waiting)      |
| **Age eligibility**    | 65+ (aged), any age (blind/disabled)   | Under full retirement age              |
| **2023 max individual**| $914/month (federal only)              | $3,627/month (maximum)                 |
| **Resource limits**    | $2,000 / $3,000                        | None                                   |

## 5. Key Statistics (Calendar Year 2023)

### Recipient Population

As of December 2023
([SSI Annual Statistical Report, 2023](https://www.ssa.gov/policy/docs/statcomps/ssi_asr/2023/sect01.html)):

- **Total SSI recipients**: **7,425,331** received federally
  administered payments.
- **Aged 65 or older**: 2,402,843 (32.4%)
- **Blind and disabled, aged 18-64**: 4,039,319 (54.4%)
- **Blind and disabled, under 18**: 983,169 (13.2%)
- 84% were eligible on the basis of blindness or disability.
- 52% were female.

### Program Costs

From the
[SSI Annual Statistical Report, 2023](https://www.ssa.gov/policy/docs/statcomps/ssi_asr/2023/index.html):

- **Federal SSI payments (CY 2023)**: **$58.2 billion**
- **Total payments including federally administered state
  supplements**: **~$61.4 billion** (a 6.6% increase from 2022)
- **Federally administered state supplementation**: $3.2 billion

### Average Benefits

- **Average monthly payment (December 2023)**: **$675**

### Concurrent Beneficiaries

Approximately **2.5 million** SSI recipients also received Social
Security (OASDI) benefits in 2023
([SSI ASR 2023, Section 3](https://www.ssa.gov/policy/docs/statcomps/ssi_asr/2023/sect03.html)).

### Suggested Benchmarking Comparisons

When comparing microsimulation results from datasets imputed with
each of the five models (QRF, OLS, Quantile Regression, Statistical
Matching, MDN), the following comparisons against administrative
totals are most informative:

1. **Total recipient count**: Compare simulated SSI take-up to the
   7,425,331 administrative total. Differences reflect how each
   imputation model's wealth distribution affects who passes the
   resource test.
2. **Total federal expenditures**: Compare simulated aggregate SSI
   benefits to the $58.2 billion administrative total.
3. **Average monthly benefit**: Compare simulated average to the
   $675 administrative average.
4. **Sanity check**: No individual's federal SSI benefit should
   exceed $914/month (individual FBR) or $1,371/month (couple FBR).

**Note on calendar year vs. fiscal year**: The SSA reports SSI
statistics on a calendar-year basis. CBO and the federal budget use
fiscal years (October-September). The figures above are calendar
year 2023 unless otherwise noted.

**Note on point-in-time vs. annual**: Recipient counts are
point-in-time (December 2023). Payment totals are annual cumulative
figures for calendar year 2023.

## 7. Policy Significance

### SSI as an Anti-Poverty Program

SSI plays a critical role in the U.S. safety net:

1. **Poverty reduction**: CBPP estimates SSI lifted approximately
   3.2 million people out of deep poverty in 2022, including roughly
   1.1 million children
   ([CBPP, 2023](https://www.cbpp.org/research/social-security/supplemental-security-income)).

2. **Gateway to health coverage**: In most states, SSI eligibility
   confers automatic Medicaid eligibility.

3. **Below-poverty floor**: The maximum federal benefit ($914/month
   in 2023) falls below the federal poverty level ($1,215/month for
   an individual in 2023), meaning SSI alone does not bring
   recipients to the poverty line.

### Ongoing Policy Debates

- **Outdated resource limits**: The $2,000/$3,000 limits have not
  been updated since 1989 (~$4,800/$7,200 in 2023 dollars).
- **Benefit adequacy**: The FBR is approximately 75% of the federal
  poverty guideline.
- **Marriage penalty**: Two SSI recipients' combined couple benefit
  ($1,371) is less than two individual benefits ($1,828).

### Relevance to Microsimulation

SSI is particularly important for microsimulation models because:

- Its means-tested structure requires detailed information about
  individual income **and assets**, making accurate data imputation
  critical.
- SSI interacts with many other programs (Social Security, SNAP,
  Medicaid, housing assistance).
- The CPS does not capture wealth/asset data, which is essential for
  modeling the SSI resource test. Imputing wealth from the SCF
  enables more accurate SSI microsimulation.
- Policy proposals to modify SSI parameters (e.g., raising resource
  limits, changing income disregards) require microsimulation to
  estimate costs and distributional effects.

## 8. PolicyEngine SSI Model Architecture

PolicyEngine US implements SSI as a chain of person-level variables
under `policyengine_us/variables/gov/ssa/ssi/`. The calculation
flows as follows:

```
ssi (final benefit, Person, YEAR)
 └─ uncapped_ssi = ssi_amount_if_eligible - ssi_countable_income
      │
      ├─ ssi_amount_if_eligible (FBR lookup)
      │    ├─ Joint claim (both spouses eligible) → couple rate / 2
      │    ├─ Spousal deeming applies → couple rate (capped to
      │    │   individual FBR in ssi.py per 20 CFR §416.1163)
      │    └─ Otherwise → individual rate
      │
      ├─ ssi_countable_income
      │    ├─ ssi_marital_earned_income
      │    │    └─ Sources: employment_income, self_employment_income
      │    ├─ ssi_marital_unearned_income
      │    │    └─ Sources: pension_income, social_security,
      │    │       disability_benefits, veterans_benefits,
      │    │       workers_compensation, unemployment_compensation,
      │    │       retirement_distributions, gi_cash_assistance,
      │    │       alimony_income, child_support_received,
      │    │       dividend_income, interest_income, rental_income
      │    ├─ ssi_blind_or_disabled_working_student_exclusion
      │    ├─ ssi_unearned_income_deemed_from_ineligible_parent
      │    ├─ ssi_income_deemed_from_ineligible_spouse
      │    └─ _apply_ssi_exclusions():
      │         1. $20/mo general exclusion (unearned first)
      │         2. $65/mo earned income exclusion
      │         3. 50% of remaining earned income excluded
      │
      └─ is_ssi_eligible
           ├─ is_ssi_aged_blind_disabled
           │    ├─ is_ssi_aged (age >= 65)
           │    ├─ is_blind
           │    └─ is_ssi_disabled (reported SSI receipt OR
           │       is_disabled, AND not engaged in SGA)
           ├─ meets_ssi_resource_test
           │    └─ ssi_countable_resources (INPUT VARIABLE,
           │       no formula — must be provided in data)
           │       Limit: $2,000 individual / $3,000 couple
           └─ citizenship (CITIZEN or qualified noncitizen)
```

**Key finding for imputation research**: The variable
`ssi_countable_resources` is a stub with no formula — it must be
provided as input data. Since the CPS does not collect wealth/asset
data, PolicyEngine uses a `pass_rate` calibration parameter of
**0.4** (meaning it probabilistically assumes only 40% of
categorically eligible people pass the asset test when resources
are unavailable). Imputing wealth from the SCF directly populates
this variable, enabling a proper resource test rather than relying
on the 40% heuristic. Different imputation models will produce
different wealth distributions, directly affecting who passes the
resource test and therefore the simulated recipient count and total
expenditures.
